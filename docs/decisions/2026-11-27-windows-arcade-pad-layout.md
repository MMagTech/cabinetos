# Windows arcade games' buttons on a pad as in the other arcade games

- **Decided:** In a Windows arcade game whose profile in windowsarcadeloader gives a pad no layout of its own, a player on a pad gets the cabinet's buttons where the console's other arcade systems put them: buttons 1 to 4 on A, B, X and Y, 5 to 8 on L1, R1, L2 and R2 (Xbox names, by position). A game whose profile sets any of a pad's face buttons, shoulders or triggers keeps the loader's layout, and so does a player on a fight stick the console knows; each player is judged alone. Start, Select, the stick clicks, the d-pad, the sticks, the pause and rumble are untouched. One rule for every game, read from the loader's profiles in CI, never a list kept by hand.
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Why:** The loader lays a pad out as a fight stick is wired (the top row, buttons 1 to 4, on X, Y, R1 and L1, the bottom row on A, B, R2 and L2), so on a pad button 1 was X: Contra shot on X and jumped on Y, where every other arcade game here has button 1 on A. The console's arcade systems agree on buttons 1 to 4 and differ on 5 to 8: this order is MAME 2003-Plus's whole and MAME 0.289's for 1 to 6, and FinalBurn Neo's generic games have 5 and 6 the other way round. On a known stick that layout is already the cabinet's, and a game whose profile lays out a pad was laid out for one, so both are left as the loader has them. The turn is made in the console's virtual controllers, not in the loader's settings layer: that layer is read last, so it would undo the games' own layouts, and it cannot tell one player from another.
- **Numbers:** #762, "Windows Arcade: games from Windows-based arcade boards, in windowsarcadeloader"; #764, "Arcade: Windows arcade board games play in windowsarcadeloader (#762)".
