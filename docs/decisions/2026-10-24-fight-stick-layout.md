# A fight stick the console knows gets an arcade layout

- **Decided:** The 8BitDo Arcade Stick, over Bluetooth in X mode, plays FBNeo and MAME 2003-Plus games with one fixed layout, a Vewlix panel's: the game's buttons in the game's order, the top row then the bottom, so a fighting game's punches are on top and its kicks below, light to heavy left to right. Four buttons take the left square; one to three take the bottom row, with a gap before the third. Its Home opens the pause menu in every game. Every other pad, and the stick in every other game, plays exactly as before: no setting, no screen. A stick is known only from a table of sticks measured on a console, never guessed.
- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Why:** A stick's eight buttons are a cabinet's panel; read by position like a pad's, a game's buttons land wherever its RetroPad ids fall, not in the cabinet's rows. Nobody without a stick may notice anything, so the rule keys on the stick itself. Over Bluetooth in X mode the stick carries the Xbox One S pad's own id, 045e:02e0, and SDL calls it an Xbox One S Controller; only the kernel's name for its device, "8BitDo Arcade Stick", tells it apart, so the table matches the id and that name, never SDL's. Measured on the A9 and rejected as ways to know it: on a cable in X mode it is a plain 045e:028e "Microsoft X-Box 360 pad" (maker "8BitDo", product "Controller"), the same as other pads; in S mode it is a Switch Pro Controller to everything, and the kernel could not start it over USB; its 2.4G receiver, 2dc8:3207 "8BitDo Arcade Stick Receiver", was not measured with the stick on it. In those it plays as a pad. A stick has no stick clicks, so the pause pair cannot be pressed, and a tap of Home is the way in, as on a stream. MAME 2003-Plus declares a game's buttons in RetroPad id order, so their order comes from its Classic table at our pin; FBNeo declares them in the game's order.
